## #96 nfl-props.js:645 — Over/Under pairing key omits book, cross-pairing multi-book quotes — CONFIRMED (P2), with a mechanism nuance

- server/services/nfl-props.js:645 `const k = [m.market, normalizeName(m.player), m.line].join('|');` — exact match. Lines 646-650 confirmed as cited: first-seen row initializes the pair object (including its `book` field via `{...m}`), subsequent rows for the same key only overwrite `e.over`/`e.under`, never `e.book`.
- `odds-api.js`'s `flattenProps()` (used by `propBoard`, not `flattenAllProps` used by the CLV capture path) already collapses to "best price per side" across ALL books before this pairing step even runs (line 166-187, keyed by `[market, player, side, point]`, explicitly documented: "keeping the best available price across books"). So by the time `market` reaches the `pairs` loop, there is normally only one row per (market, player, line, side) — meaning the true mechanism isn't "duplicate multi-book rows for the same side colliding on the join key" (adding `book` to the key wouldn't change anything, since there's nothing to disambiguate at that point) — it's that the Over-best-price and Under-best-price can each legitimately come from *different* books, and the merged pair object then carries only ONE of those books' identity in its `book` field while reporting a devigged probability derived from both.
- Net effect matches the claim's stated impact exactly: `noVig(m.over, m.under)` (nfl-prop-props.js area, called downstream) computes an implied probability from what may be two different books' one-sided prices as if it were one coherent two-sided market, and the displayed `book: m.book` (line 674) can misattribute that blended computation to whichever book happened to post first for that key.
- Verdict: CONFIRMED (the observable defect — wrong/misleading book label and a devig computed across mismatched books — is real and reproducible from the code), but the root cause is one layer upstream (`flattenProps`'s intentional book-discarding "best price" collapse) rather than purely "the pairing key omits book" as literally stated. Kept as CONFIRMED since the practical claim (wrong implied probability, wrong book label when several books are fetched) holds up; noted the mechanism nuance for the record.
