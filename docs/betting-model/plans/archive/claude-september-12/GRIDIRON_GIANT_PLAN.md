## Section 0 — Headline verdict

Gridiron is a codebase where the parts that matter least are the most careful and the parts that matter most are the messiest, and this is now three independent efforts agreeing, not two. The boot/platform/DB spine, the fantasy player-model core, the draft/trade layer, and the audit/replay machinery are rated *acceptable* — walk-forward, honestly self-documented, cutoff-safe by construction almost everywhere it counts. The market-data layer, the execution/money path, the client betting surface, and the props pipeline are rated *messy-to-broken*. The research sweep's independent read of the same codebase reaches the same shape from the outside: six defects it calls "load-bearing and cheap" (away-team win-probability sign, a coin-flip home-field lump, a timeout that's computed but never spent, five disagreeing CLV formulas, an ensemble that's 20 models but really ~3 independent signals, and a fantasy cold-start loop that has *never once* captured a Week-1 forward snapshot) line up almost exactly with what the audit already found by reading the files directly — in three cases (the receipt-clock bug, the away-team WP sign bug, and the weekly-learning cold-start bug) the two efforts cite the *identical line number* independently. That agreement between AUDIT and the research sweep is the strongest evidence in this whole corpus that the P1 list is real, not an artifact of one team's methodology — and, for the market-data/audit-replay layer specifically, the next paragraph is a *third*, later, independently-run and adversarially-verified effort reaching the identical "messy-to-broken" verdict a third way, from a from-scratch read of the same codebase done the same night as the other two.

**AUDIT-SYSTEM's own headline verdict, independently reached from a dedicated deep-dive into the audit/replay system alone, sharpens this to a number — the third convergence, not a restatement of the first two:** "Gridiron has two audit systems and neither is currently producing admissible betting evidence... As of tonight the number of admissible 2026 forward observations is zero" (AUDIT-SYSTEM §0, `audit-system/AUDIT_SYSTEM_REVIEW.md:30-46`). The historical system audits a forecast that isn't served (`blendMode 'raw'` in replay vs. `'market_residual'` on the live board — AUDIT-SYSTEM G05) and grades at the nflverse closing consensus because the decision-time column (`closing_spread`) is NULL on every 2021-25 row, not because anyone chose to (AUDIT-SYSTEM G07 — note this is a different "G07" than Section 1's drive-simulator reader group of the same name); the forward system's T-60 packet → tape → ledger → CLV → findings chain is a set of well-tested modules that are not wired to each other in production — every live quote batch still carries the legacy request clock (AUDIT-SYSTEM G01, same defect as this plan's Section 3.2, now with an exact denominator: 1,323/1,323 live batches), Week 1 decisions were written to a mutable UPSERT table instead of the append-only tape (AUDIT-SYSTEM G03), and the one 2026 ledger with real rows recorded two already-finished games as forward predictions (AUDIT-SYSTEM G04). This independently corroborates, with harder numbers, the same "market-data layer is messy-to-broken" verdict this section already reached from the outside — see Section 8.9-8.17 for the full merge of AUDIT-SYSTEM's findings, and Section 7.0 for the sample-size arithmetic behind why 2026 alone cannot decide a profitability claim either way, regardless of any of tonight's fixes.

What's urgent right now, tonight, is small: a boot-time reconcile job (`server/db/seed/index.js:27`) is silently overwriting draft-board ranks and AI team analyses on every restart; a tracked 1KB file carries an ESPN session-cookie-bearing database fragment and is about to go out in a push of 58 unpushed commits; 13 route families sit unauthenticated behind a real, documented, recurring phone-tunnel workflow; and the one strategy the project believes is +EV (the Wong teaser) can have its settlement ledger forged by an unauthenticated caller. None of these are hard to fix — every one is a same-day, few-line change with a stated exit test. What's genuinely "rebuild, not patch" work is narrower than it sounds: the ensemble measurably underperforms the market it wraps (RMSE 12.840 vs. 12.448, t=4.47) because it's mostly the same ~3 signals wearing 31 different-looking hats, and fixing that for real — not painting over it — needs a team-strength refit, a real forecast-combination test, and a governed way to know if any of it actually helped. The betting engine's headline finding stands unchanged by any of tonight's work: no edge exists against the closing line, measured three independent times, and the only positive number anywhere in the betting stack is execution/line-shopping — which itself cannot currently see the one book that produces most of its edge.

**MLB, named here so it isn't only findable by triangulating three sections (AUDIT's own Section A had the identical gap — CRITIC Gap 5):** MLB odds capture has been off since 2026-09-01 (a credit-drain guard that was never revisited) and is still off today — ~1,300 boxscore fetches a day are being spent on `mlb_logs`/`mlb_tomorrow_picks` for zero downstream picks. ## Section 3 — Phase: Data-integrity / silent-corruption fixes

Things silently overwriting or corrupting data **right now**, live, during Week 1 capture.

### 3.1 — Seed-on-boot data loss
**`server/db/seed/index.js:27`**, called unconditionally from `server/index.js:58` (AUDIT D.1 #1). Misnamed: runs on *every* boot, not just an empty DB, and `ON CONFLICT ... DO UPDATE`s static editorial text and default draft-board ranks over live data. **Impact:** overwrites AI-generated team analyses (`routes/analysis.js:81`), user team edits (`routes/teams.js:108`), ESPN head-coach sync (`routes/nfldata.js:97`), and the user's own draft-board ordering (`routes/rankings.js:19,53`) on every restart — confirmed live, `nfl_teams.analysis_updated_at` timestamps within the last day sit next to text that will revert on the next boot. **Fix:** `INSERT OR IGNORE` for teams, create-if-absent for the ranking set, never `DO UPDATE` editorial/rank columns; or gate behind an explicit `--reseed` flag. **Exit test:** restart the server twice; a manually-edited team analysis and a manually-reordered board rank both survive both restarts. *Single source: AUDIT only — no research-sweep overlap (this is a Gridiron-specific application bug, not a modeled-literature question).*

### 3.2 — [MERGED] The T-60 receipt-clock bug
**Sources:** AUDIT D.1 #6 (`server/services/book-feeds.js:417`, `server/services/line-shopping.js:66`), AUDIT §I item 3; **FIX #42** (F05-bitemporal-pit, same file, `book-feeds.js:390-419`); **AUDIT-SYSTEM G01** (identical two lines, adversarially **CONFIRMED** on all three legs 2026-09-12 — `audit-system/AUDIT_SYSTEM_REVIEW.md:208,252`).

**AUDIT-SYSTEM's independent live-DB re-check gives this the hardest number in the whole corpus:** `nfl_quote_batches.receipt_clock_source = 'legacy_request_time_only'` on **1,323 of 1,323** live batches — not "most," all of them — re-confirmed read-only against the live database on 2026-09-11/12 while the capture server was running (`AUDIT_SYSTEM_REVIEW.md:14`). The verifier's own words: "the only `response_completion` producer (`nfl-quote-tape.js:127-137`) is reachable solely from a manual route, and `nfl-t60-packet.js:214-216` admits nothing else — zero eligible prices have ever existed" (`:252`). This is now a three-way independent hit (AUDIT, FIX #42, AUDIT-SYSTEM) on the identical mechanism, the third arrived at by a from-scratch three-lens adversarial review rather than a second read of the same code.

Both documents independently arrive at the identical line and the identical mechanism: the only two running quote-tape producers compute a genuine post-response timestamp but never pass it as `receivedAt` — every batch is stamped `legacy_request_time_only`. **Impact (AUDIT):** `nfl-t60-packet.js:213-217` can only grant `received_by_cutoff` to a `response_completion`-clocked quote, so **every prospective T-60 packet — including the one already frozen for SF@LAR — is structurally unable to contain a single knowable quote.** This zeroes the entire prospective-evidence experiment the 2026-09-10 session spent real effort building. **Impact (FIX #42, independently):** "every batch this feed has ever written, including this weekend's live Week 1 captures, is permanently labeled `legacy_request_time_only` and discarded by the T-60 packet's `realClock` filter." **Fix (both sources agree):** capture `requestedAt` before the `Promise.all`, pass the post-await timestamp as `receivedAt` with `receiptClockSource:'response_completion'`. **Exit test:** the next frozen T-60 packet reports at least one `received_by_cutoff` quote — FIX #42 adds the time pressure explicitly: "check before Sunday's games settle, not after." **This is a one-line-shaped fix confirmed independently by two completely different methodologies (a code auditor and a literature-grounded researcher). Do it today, not this week.**

The related, narrower defect at **`nfl-t60-packet.js:258`** (AUDIT D.1 #7, no exit test given in AUDIT, supplied here; **third citation: AUDIT-SYSTEM G31**, part of a broader finding — see Section 8.14): injury rows are granted `received_by_cutoff` on nflverse's `modified_at` publish clock, not a receipt clock — a historical replay in "prospective" mode counts injury reports fetched years later as knowable at the time. **AUDIT-SYSTEM's own framing sharpens why this specific line matters more than it looks:** on adversarial review this was called out as "worse than stated" — `modified_at` is written from the CSV's own `date_modified` (`nfl-advanced.js:306-311`), a pure publication clock, and "that clock grants `received_by_cutoff`, **the one claim a prospective packet may rest on**" (`AUDIT_SYSTEM_REVIEW.md:273`) — i.e., this is not one of several receipt-clock problems, it is currently the *only* path by which the forward packet can claim anything was knowable in time, and it's wrong. **Fix:** use `nfl_injuries`' own fetch/receipt stamp if one exists, else classify as `published_by_cutoff_evidenced` (historical-only). **Exit test:** a replay run against a game where the real injury report postdated the fetch timestamp shows the injury correctly excluded from `received_by_cutoff`.

### 3.3 — [MERGED-family] The opening-spread corruption chain
**Source: AUDIT only** (no research-sweep overlap — this is Gridiron's own multi-source data-provenance mess, not a modeled-literature question). Grouped here because three separate defects share one root symptom and one fix order.

1. **Root cause — `server/services/odds-archive.js:138-142`** (AUDIT D.1 #14): the archive-median opener backfill computes a single home-perspective median and writes it, unmodified, to *both* the home and away row via one `UPDATE ... WHERE ((team=? AND opponent=?) OR (team=? AND opponent=?))`. **Impact:** every 2022–2025 `open_spread` value is corrupted — antisymmetry check confirms 5/267, 4/285, 1/285, 4/285 pairs correct for 2022–2025 vs. 256/256, 251/251, 272/272, and 271/271 for 2019–2021 and live-captured 2026. Apparent line movement is inflated roughly 1.0→5.3 points/game for any analysis using `open_spread` from 2022 onward, and no prior document connects this cause to this effect. **Fix:** re-derive backfilled openers per-row with the away side negated (validated: median |difference| = 0.00 on n=347 when done correctly); add a provenance column distinguishing backfilled from natively-captured openers. **Exit test (AUDIT §I item 9):** the antisymmetry check on 2022–2025 `open_spread` returns near-100% correct pairs after the fix.
2. **Downstream symptom #1 — `nfl-ensemble.js:60,602,1247`** (AUDIT D.1 #15, no exit test given in AUDIT, supplied here): `games()` hard-codes `NULL AS open_spread` for every historical/backtest row, so `market_anchor`'s live number for an unplayed game is a *different quantity* than the one its weight and residual slope were fit on. **Impact:** for every unplayed game, the highest-weighted raw-blend component quietly injects the opener into a blend calibrated on the close — the memory-recorded finding that "the opener path has zero measured CLV for this model" is a direct, previously-unexplained consequence of this line. **Fix:** `COALESCE` `open_spread` into `games()` so the anchor is graded on what it actually predicts live, or drop the opener branch entirely. **Exit test:** re-run the RMSE-vs-market comparison after the fix; confirm `market_anchor`'s in-sample and live-serving inputs are now the same quantity (a synthetic fixture where opener≠close should show a measurable RMSE change if the branch is live at all).
3. **Downstream symptom #2 — `nfl-opening-lines.js:264-333`, `odds-archive.js`** (AUDIT D.1 #16, no exit test given in AUDIT, supplied here): `game_lines.open_spread` is one column stitched from four incompatible sources across three eras (Westgate SuperContest 2013–2020; nflverse `initial_lines.csv` for 2021 alone, 3.40-point average error vs. Pinnacle's real opener, 139/272 games moving ≥3 points; the corrupted 2022–2025 archive median from item 1 above; live ESPN openers for 2026). **Impact:** matches Pinnacle's real archived opener only ~40% of the time across comparable seasons; every "beats the opener" measurement is unsafe. **Fix:** label each row with its source era; do not compute a pooled opener statistic across eras without reconciling them first. **Exit test:** a per-era antisymmetry/accuracy check is reported separately for each of the four source eras, and no report computes a single pooled "opener accuracy" number across them.

### 6.2 — [MERGED] Home-field-advantage post-OT lump
**Sources:** AUDIT D.2 (`server/services/nfl-drive-sim.js:562`); **FIX #10** (score 14.3, 3/3); **CATALOG** (`martineastwood/penaltyblog`, `greerreNFL/nfelohfa`, `fivethirtyeight/nfl-elo-game`; build-list items 7–8).

### 7.0 — [NEW] What a season can and cannot decide (AUDIT-SYSTEM §5 metrics)
