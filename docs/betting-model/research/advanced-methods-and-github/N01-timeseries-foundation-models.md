# N01 — Time-Series Foundation Models for Gridiron (Chronos / TimesFM / Moirai / Lag-Llama)

Researcher: N01-timeseries-foundation-models. Bucket: new (capability). Date: 2026-09-12.

## Sources read in full

### 1. Chronos: Learning the Language of Time Series (Ansari et al., TMLR 10/2024)
arXiv:2403.07815v3. Read: pp.1-9 (abstract, intro, related work, method §3-4, benchmark setup §5.1-5.2) via full PDF.

- **Model**: T5 encoder-decoder family, 4 sizes 20M(Mini)/46M(Small)/200M(Base)/710M(Large), plus a GPT-2 decoder-only variant. Time series values are mean-scaled then quantized into a fixed vocabulary of 4096 tokens; the model is trained with plain cross-entropy (classification, not regression) — no time-series-specific architecture at all.
- **Data**: 28 datasets, ~890K series, ~84B pretraining tokens/observations, split Pretraining-only (13 sets/795,936 series), Benchmark I in-domain (15 sets/97,272 series), Benchmark II zero-shot (27 sets/190,674 series). Augmented with TSMixup (convex combinations of real series) and KernelSynth (Gaussian-process synthetic series) — real data alone was judged insufficient.
- **Result (honest)**: on the 27-dataset zero-shot benchmark, Chronos is "comparable and occasionally superior" to methods trained specifically on those datasets — not uniformly better, and it wins decisively only on datasets that were literally in its training corpus (Benchmark I). Sample: these are retail/energy/web/finance/healthcare/transport series at frequencies from 5 minutes to yearly; the paper does not evaluate anything resembling a 17-point season.
- Context length 512 tokens (with EOS/PAD); quantization confines the prediction range to the observed training interval, which the authors flag as "theoretically infeasible to model time series with a strong trend."

### 2. A decoder-only foundation model for time-series forecasting (TimesFM; Das, Kong, Sen, Zhou — Google Research, 2024)
arXiv:2310.10688v4. Read in full, pp.1-9 (through Conclusion/Impact Statement).

- **Model**: 200M-parameter decoder-only transformer, input patch 32 / output patch 128 (longer output than input patch — deliberate one-shot multi-step decoding rather than pure autoregression), causal self-attention, point-forecast MSE loss (no native probabilistic head in the base model).
- **Data**: O(100B) real time points — mostly Google Trends (22k queries, 2007-2022) and Wikipedia pageviews (2012-2023, ~300B raw points before filtering), plus all of M4, and Electricity/Traffic/Weather from the long-horizon benchmark, plus 3M synthetic (ARMA/trend/seasonal/step) series of 2048 points each, mixed 80% real / 20% synthetic.
- **Critical honesty finding for this pilot**: *"For weekly granularity we do not have sufficiently long time series; therefore a maximum context length of 256 is used [instead of 512]."* Even Google's own pretraining corpus, built from 15+ years of daily web-search and pageview data, judged weekly-frequency series too short at native scale and had to shrink the context window — and "sufficiently long" there still means on the order of years of weekly points, nowhere near the 17 points in one NFL season. This is the single strongest piece of primary-source evidence that these models were built and evaluated for regimes far outside the target application.
- **Result (honest)**: zero-shot MAE (scaled to a naive baseline) is best-in-class on Monash (beats N-BEATS "within significance," beats DeepAR and WaveNet clearly) and competitive-but-not-dominant on Darts (within significance of ARIMA and llmtime, not clearly better — the paper's own words: "since there are only 8 individual time-series in this dataset group, the standard errors are not sharp and do not provide a clear ordering"). On Informer/ETT long-horizon tasks (hourly/15-min electricity-transformer data, i.e., very high-frequency, high-N series) TimesFM plus PatchTST (supervised) are the two best models. All three benchmark groups are high-frequency/high-N; none tests short weekly panels.

### 3. Unified Training of Universal Time Series Forecasting Transformers (Moirai; Woo, Liu, Kumar, Xiong, Savarese, Sahoo — Salesforce, ICML 2024 oral)
arXiv:2402.02592v2. Read in full, pp.1-7 (through ablation intro).

- **Model**: masked-encoder Transformer, 3 sizes — Small 14M / Base 91M / Large 311M — with "Any-variate Attention" (handles arbitrary numbers of covariates via binary attention biases, not fixed input dimension) and a 4-component mixture output (Student-t, negative-binomial, log-normal, low-variance normal) so it natively produces calibrated predictive distributions, not just point forecasts.
- **Data**: LOTSA, 27.6B observations across 9 domains (Energy 59%, Transport 18%, Climate 15%, CloudOps, Web, Sales, Nature, Econ/Fin, Healthcare), spanning yearly through multi-second frequency; weekly data is only 0.067% of LOTSA (18.5M of 27.6B observations) — the single most under-represented frequency band after "second-level" (0.054%).
- **Result (honest)**: on 6 zero-shot probabilistic-forecasting datasets (Electricity, Solar, Walmart, Weather, Istanbul Traffic, Turkey Power — again all high-frequency/high-N), Moirai-Base/Large get best-or-second-best CRPS in 4/6 and are "still close to the best performing" on the 2 it loses (Walmart, Istanbul Traffic) versus baselines individually trained on each target set. On long-sequence ETT/Electricity/Weather benchmarks it's competitive with, not uniformly better than, supervised iTransformer/PatchTST. Minimum sequence length per variate during pretraining task sampling was set to just 2 time-steps — i.e., the architecture is technically built to tolerate very short series inside a pooled multi-series training regime, which is the structural fact this pilot leans on (see Candidate 1).

### 4. Lag-Llama: Towards Foundation Models for Probabilistic Time Series Forecasting (Rasul, Ashok, et al. — ServiceNow/Mila, 2024)
arXiv:2310.08278v3. Read in full, pp.1-8 (through Discussion).

- **Model**: decoder-only, LLaMA backbone (RMSNorm, RoPE), tokenizes each timestep as a vector of *lagged values* at a fixed set of lag indices (quarterly/monthly/weekly/daily/hourly/second-level lags, e.g. lag-1, lag-7, lag-14, …) plus calendar covariates. Outputs a Student-t distribution per step.
- **Structural constraint directly relevant here**: the tokenization "requires an L-sized or larger context window," where L is the *largest* lag index used (the paper's own caveat, §4.1 footnote). For weekly-frequency lag features that includes a 52-week (yearly-seasonal) lag by design — meaning the model's own input scheme cannot be fed a single team's 17-week season at all; there is no way to construct a lag-52 feature from 17 points. This is the most direct disqualifier of the four papers for the naive "one team, one season" application Nick might otherwise assume is the obvious use case.
- **Data**: 27 datasets, 7,965 series, ~352M training windows/tokens, across energy/transportation/economics/nature/air-quality/cloud-ops.
- **Result (honest, and the least flattering of the four)**: zero-shot average rank **6.714 out of 13 methods** (Table 1) on 7 held-out datasets — worse (higher/worse rank) than several baselines trained from scratch per-dataset (PatchTST 5.643, N-BEATS 5.0, TFT 5.0, DeepAR 5.714). Zero-shot Lag-Llama is not reliably better than simple supervised methods; it only becomes state-of-the-art (**rank 2.786**) after *fine-tuning* on the target dataset, and few-shot experiments (Table 2) still assume 20-80% of a dataset with dozens-to-hundreds of points per series, not a handful. This paper is the clearest evidence that "zero-shot" claims for this model class do not transfer to genuinely tiny series without adaptation data.

### Secondary/orienting search (not read in full, used only to confirm no existing sports application and to corroborate the short-series limitation independently)
- WebSearch confirms no published work applies Chronos/TimesFM/Moirai/Lag-Llama to NFL/NBA or any sports panel — this is genuinely unclaimed territory, consistent with Nick's "new capability" framing.
- A practitioner summary (aihorizonforecast.substack.com, not primary, treated as corroborating color only) states plainly: "In extreme cases where you have yearly, quarterly, or monthly data with very few datapoints, foundation models may not perform well because the context length is not enough to capture any meaningful behavior" — same conclusion the primary papers already support via TimesFM's weekly-context reduction and Lag-Llama's lag-window requirement.

## Honest verdict for a 17-week NFL season

1. **None of the four papers evaluate anything close to a 17-observation series.** The shortest-frequency data any of them handles at scale is weekly, and even there (TimesFM) the authors judged their own multi-year weekly panels "not sufficiently long" and cut context to 256. A single team's single-season weekly stat line (17 points, fewer once byes/injuries are dropped) is roughly 1-2 orders of magnitude shorter than anything in any of these benchmarks. Zero-shotting a raw single-team-season series with any of these four models is out-of-distribution in a way the authors themselves would flag.
2. **The way these architectures actually cope with short individual series is cross-sectional pooling, not longer context** — Moirai explicitly trains with per-variate windows as short as 2 steps, *because* it treats a "dataset" as thousands of such short/medium series pooled together and shares statistical strength across them (that's the entire "universal forecaster" premise). This is exploitable: Gridiron already has the pooled panel this needs — every team-week and player-week is a row of a wide multi-season table (`nfl_team_week_features`, `nfl_player_week_features`, `player_week_usage`), so "one series" for a TSFM pilot should be one team-stat-season (17 points) stacked across ~32 teams x 5-6 seasons (2021-2026) = 150-190 short series per stat, which is a completely ordinary "many short series" panel of exactly the kind Moirai/Chronos are pretrained to forecast, and squarely inside Lag-Llama's few-shot-adaptation regime (Table 2 shows it fine-tunes usefully with only 20% of a target dataset).
3. **Zero-shot is not free lunch even in-distribution.** Lag-Llama's own zero-shot rank (6.714/13) is worse than several from-scratch supervised baselines; TimesFM is only "within significance" (not clearly better) on the Darts group; Chronos is only "comparable, occasionally superior" out of training-corpus. The realistic expectation for a Gridiron pilot is therefore: zero-shot gives a free, no-training-cost *baseline forecaster and calibrated uncertainty band* worth comparing against the hand-fit ensemble — not an edge to expect on day one. Any real gain almost certainly requires the fine-tuning/few-shot step these papers show actually moves the needle (Lag-Llama rank 2.786 fine-tuned vs 6.714 zero-shot).
4. **Practical/infra reality check**: `research/requirements.txt` (Python 3.11-3.13, isolated env, no app-runtime dependency) currently has no `torch`/`transformers`/`gluonts` — running any of Chronos, TimesFM, or Moirai (all PyTorch, 14M-710M params, open weights on HuggingFace under Apache-2.0, no paid API involved) means adding those to the isolated research venv. That's a same-night, hours-scale addition for CPU inference on a small variant (Chronos-Small/Bolt-small or Moirai-Small); a laptop can run inference on a few hundred short series in minutes. Fine-tuning is a heavier days-scale step and should be scoped separately after the zero-shot pilot's exit test.
5. **Governance fit**: any output of this pilot that is meant to reach a market model (spread/total/props) must go through `model-governance.js`'s registered-feature-contract pattern — exactly the same gate `nfl-team-strength.js` describes for its own team-level aggregates ("route it through model-governance.js as a registered feature contract, starting blocked. Not as a quietly-added column"). A TSFM signal is not exempt from that discipline just because it's novel.
