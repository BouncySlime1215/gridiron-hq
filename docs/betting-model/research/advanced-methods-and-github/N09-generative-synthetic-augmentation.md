# N09 — Generative models (diffusion/VAE) for synthetic play/drive-sequence augmentation on small-n sports data

## Question
Does synthetic data (VAE/GAN/diffusion-generated) genuinely help small-sample sports models, or does it leak/overfit and just launder the same small sample back into the model? If credible, specify a bounded pilot on Gridiron's smallest-sample surface.

## Bottom line
The sports-specific generative-trajectory literature (basketball, tennis, soccer) is real and produces convincing output, but every one of those systems is trained on **hundreds of thousands to millions of sequences** (107K-500K basketball/soccer possessions, 7.6M tennis rallies) — the opposite of Gridiron's small-n problem. None of them demonstrate the thing Gridiron actually needs: generating useful synthetic rows from a *few dozen to low-hundreds* real examples. The tabular small-sample literature is the closer analog, and it is genuinely double-edged: augmentation measurably helps prognostic models on datasets with <200 rows in a controlled study (avg +15.6% relative AUC), but a separate honest evaluation of tiny tabular datasets (9-30 rows) found that standard fidelity metrics (propensity score, MMD) can look fine while the synthetic data is topologically nothing like the real distribution — i.e., the metrics you'd use to sign off on a Gridiron pilot are exactly the ones shown to lie at small n. Verdict: **credible but narrow** — worth a small, falsifiable pilot on Gridiron's smallest and most survivorship-biased table (`nfl_rookie_evidence`, n=75), not a general architecture change, and never on anything used for betting decisions given the token/compute-is-cheap-but-trust-is-not asymmetry.

## Primary sources read in full

### 1. Zhan, Zheng, Yue, Sha, Lucey — "Generating Multi-Agent Trajectories using Programmatic Weak Supervision" (arXiv:1803.07612)
- **Model**: Hierarchical VRNN with a shared "macro-intent" layer, macro-intents supervised by programmatic weak labels; RNN samples macro-intents over time, per-agent VRNNs (K=5) generate full trajectories conditioned on them.
- **Data**: Basketball SportVU tracking, **107,146 training / 13,845 test sequences** of 5 offensive players over 50 timesteps (8.3s at 6Hz), half-court. A synthetic-Boids control set of 32,768/8,192 sequences.
- **Result**: Comparable log-likelihood to single/independent VRNN baselines but much better realism — 100% (25/25) analyst preference over single-VRNN, 60% (15/25, p<0.05) over independent-VRNN; out-of-bounds rate 15.5% vs 45.7% baseline; avg speed 0.79 vs ground-truth 0.77 ft/frame.
- **Honest limitation**: macro-intents are "simple domain-specific heuristics," unclear how to port to a new domain; defense and the ball were dropped for future work. Nowhere do they test or claim this works from a small sample — 107K sequences is the whole point of the architecture (it needs enough data to learn the macro-intent/agent split at all).

### 2. "SportsNGEN: Sustained Generation of Realistic Multi-player Sports Gameplay" (arXiv:2403.12977, 2024)
- **Model**: Transformer decoder (extends baller2vec) with nucleus sampling — not diffusion, despite the "generative" branding common in 2024-25 sports-ML papers.
- **Data**: Professional tennis, **~15,000 matches, 7.6M rally sequences at 25Hz**; qualitative football (soccer) extension.
- **Result**: Well-calibrated on aggregate shot/rally statistics (serve %, ace %, rally-length distribution) via Wasserstein-distance comparison to real distributions; calibrated win-probability vs ground truth, "with deviations where data are sparse" — i.e. their own calibration degrades exactly where sample size shrinks, the regime Gridiron's rookie/prop tails live in.
- **Critical engineering finding**: training on noise-free trajectories produced unstable, degenerate simulation — noise injection during training was necessary to keep long rollouts realistic. ~20% of generated rallies were unrealistic and discarded by hand. No downstream-task (train-on-synthetic-test-on-real) evaluation is reported at all — realism was judged by summary statistics only, not by whether a model trained on the synthetic data predicts real outcomes better.

### 3. Deep Generative Multi-Agent Imitation Model, football (soccer) case study (arXiv:2303.13323)
- **Model**: Conditional VRNN, prior/posterior/LSTM conditioned on CNN-LSTM-labeled context (pressing/backing/staying).
- **Data**: 43 Premier League matches → **500,000 training sequences** of 6-frame pitch-control-map snippets.
- **Result**: SSIM 0.954 (reconstruction) / 0.850 (prediction) vs 0.900/0.795 for vanilla VRNN — a real but incremental gain, evaluated only on pixel-map structural similarity, not on any wagering- or fantasy-relevant downstream metric.
- **Honest limitation**: authors state the biggest challenge is "mapping team behaviors to a demonstration which can be sampled from," give no evidence the method works below the ~500K-sequence regime, and note context-labeling itself required a separately trained classifier — another layer that needs its own training data.

### 4. "Evaluating Synthetic Tabular Data Generated To Augment Small Sample Datasets" (arXiv:2211.10760)
- **Model**: A single WGAN (nbsynthetic).
- **Data**: Four genuinely tiny tabular datasets — 11×9, 30×15, 9×7, 9×56 rows×cols.
- **Honest negative finding (the load-bearing one for this brief)**: on the 9×7 "Balls" physics dataset, propensity-score MSE was a reassuring 0.0114, yet the topological Bottleneck distance between real and synthetic distributions was 297,000 (p≈0.0002) — i.e. the standard go/no-go metric said "fine" while the data was structurally wrong. Quote: *"common metrics like propensity scoring and MMD often suggest similarity where fundamental topological differences exist."* Their own proposed fix (a normalized bottleneck metric) was itself unstable at this sample size (exceeded its theoretical bound by 18% on one dataset). Conclusion in the authors' own words: no single metric — including a purpose-built one — reliably validates synthetic tabular data at n<30. This directly undercuts any plan to validate a Gridiron rookie-augmentation pilot by "the fidelity metrics looked good."

### 5. Note on Shumailov et al. 2024 ("AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated data," Nature) — critique at arXiv:2410.12954
- Confirms the original Nature finding (iteratively training generative models on their own synthetic output degrades them — even a plain KDE-and-resample toy model shows it, so it's a statistical phenomenon, not a deep-learning-specific bug) but shows the severity is metric-dependent (KL divergence plateaus; Wasserstein distance keeps growing) and is far worse under **full replacement** of real data by synthetic each generation than under **mixing** synthetic with retained real data.
- **Practical takeaway for Gridiron**: never let a synthetic-augmented dataset become the *training set* for the next generation's generator (no recursive self-training loop), and always keep the real rows as the majority/anchor of any blended training set — never synthetic-only.

## Secondary source (abstract-level only, not counted toward the 4+ full reads)
- Liu, El Kababji et al., "Synthetic Data Generation for Augmenting Small Samples" (arXiv:2501.18741, Jan 2025) — seven small health datasets; augmentation improved AUC by an average 15.55% relative (range 4.3-43.2%) vs. resampling alone (p=0.016), with larger gains for datasets with fewer observations, lower baseline AUC, higher-cardinality categoricals, and more balanced outcomes. This is the strongest "it genuinely helps" data point found tonight, but the PDF could not be parsed in full (encoded stream extraction failed twice), so treat the numbers as abstract-sourced, not verified against the body/appendix.

## What this means for Gridiron specifically
Gridiron has no play/drive-sequence generative model today (confirmed: `server/services/nfl-drive-sim.js`, 1280 lines, is a hand-written physics/policy simulator, not a learned generative model — separately, tonight's audit found it has 6 real physics bugs, which a synthetic-augmentation project must not be layered on top of until fixed). Gridiron's actual smallest-n surface with a stated survivorship problem is `server/services/nfl-rookies.js`:

> "the n=75 historical rookie seasons behind the table above are survivors. Late-round bands are the least trustworthy for exactly this reason." (file header, nfl-rookies.js)

n=75 rookie-seasons, drawn from `nfl_rookie_evidence` (schema: server/db/schema/nfl-n-to-z.js:479), feeding a ridge-shrunk challenger (`ridgeFit`, imported from nfl-specialists.js) that has to beat a draft-capital prior. This is precisely the regime (n<100, tabular, small-sample GAN/VAE literature) the sources above actually speak to — not the play-sequence regime (which needs 10^5-10^6 rows the codebase doesn't have and the papers above show needing).

Also relevant: `server/services/player-week-engine.js` computes `heads` (season_to_date/last3/last1/median) per player-week and is null at cold start (confirmed: line 574, `if (!engine || !heads) return {...}` — the guard exists precisely because heads is null on players with no accumulated weeks, i.e. rookies in week 1). A rookie-evidence synthetic-augmentation pilot and the cold-start fix are the same defect from two angles.

Five candidates: (1) bounded VAE/bootstrap augmentation pilot on nfl_rookie_evidence (n=75), gated by a walk-forward exit test; (2) synthetic cold-start "heads" for player-week-engine.js rookies, replacing the silent null; (3) a TSTR + honest-fidelity (propensity/MMD + topological) governance gate as a new, currently-nonexistent capability, required before any synthetic data reaches a model; (4) an augmentation-applicability screen (Liu et al. 2025 criteria) to decide where in Gridiron augmentation is even worth trying; (5) replace point-estimate prop projections with calibrated predictive distributions via lightweight hierarchical/negative-binomial modeling (not a data-hungry deep generative model — the evidence above says deep generative sequence models need 10^5-10^6 rows Gridiron does not have at the per-market granularity).

## Do-not-do list (with citations)
1. Do NOT build a deep generative (VAE/GAN/diffusion) model of full play or drive sequences for nfl-drive-sim.js or the season-remainder simulator. Every real sports sequence-generation system reviewed needed 10^5-10^6 training sequences (107K basketball possessions, 500K soccer snippets, 7.6M tennis rallies) to avoid degenerate output; Gridiron's entire play-by-play table is 251,591 rows across many seasons/teams/situations — several orders of magnitude short of the regime these architectures are validated in. (Zhan et al. 1803.07612; CVRNN-football 2303.13323; SportsNGEN 2403.12977)
2. Do NOT validate any synthetic dataset with propensity-score or MMD similarity alone, especially below n≈100. arXiv:2211.10760 shows a case where propensity-MSE (0.011, "fine") coexisted with a topological distance so large it proved the distributions were fundamentally different. Any go/no-go must use multiple, disagreeing-metric-aware evaluation, not one number.
3. Do NOT let a rookie-augmentation model retrain itself on its own synthetic output in a future season ("self-distillation" / recursive loop). Shumailov et al. 2024 and the critique at 2410.12954 agree recursive synthetic-only training degrades distributions; only ever mix synthetic rows into a majority-real training set, never replace real rows with synthetic ones.
4. Do NOT ship a rookie-augmentation pilot's output anywhere near live betting stakes or lineup decisions before a forward (not backtested) evaluation period, given nfl-rookies.js's own stated survivorship bias (n=75 rookie-seasons are survivors) — synthetic rows generated from a biased sample launder the bias, they do not fix it.
5. Do NOT treat "the synthetic data looks realistic to a human/analyst" (the preference-study standard used by Zhan et al.) as sufficient for a decision-relevant model. Realism to an eye is a different property from calibration for a ridge-shrunk challenger; none of the sports papers reviewed tested train-on-synthetic-test-on-real for a betting- or fantasy-relevant downstream metric.
