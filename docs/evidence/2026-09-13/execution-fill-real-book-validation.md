# Depth-aware fill vs naive fill, measured on the real captured order books

Generated by `scripts/execution-fill-real-book-validation.mjs`, reading
`server/data.sqlite` **read-only** (`node:sqlite { readOnly: true }`, its own
connection — never through `server/db/index.js`, which opens read-write and
would sit next to the live capture process). No write of any kind. Raw output
in `execution-fill-real-book-validation.json` in this folder; rerun any time
with `node scripts/execution-fill-real-book-validation.mjs`.

## What could actually be measured

`polymarket_quotes` has 16.5M rows since 2026-08-29, but only **65,624** carry
a real two-sided touch with real resting size on both sides (`bid_size`,
`ask_size` both present and positive) — the other ~16.4M rows have null sizes.
Those 65,624 snapshots span **181 distinct markets** (NFL game/prop/futures
markets on Polymarket). That is the entire population this measurement can be
made on; nothing here is a projection or a synthetic table.

Every one of those snapshots is a **one-level** book — `polymarket_quotes` has
columns for exactly `best_bid/best_ask/bid_size/ask_size`, so this is a fill
against the touch only, capped by the touch's real size. It answers "how much
of a naive stake would the posted size have actually absorbed", not "at what
average price would the rest have filled" — the ladder past level one was
never written to disk (see the CAVEAT in `server/services/execution-fill.js`
and migration 044), so that second question stays unanswerable until capture
changes.

## The result

| stake | fully filled | mean fill ratio | median fill ratio | p10 fill ratio | naive overstates fill by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $50   | 76.2% | 0.863 | 1.000 | 0.304 | 13.7% |
| $100  | 65.0% | 0.782 | 1.000 | 0.152 | 21.8% |
| $250  | 51.2% | 0.656 | 1.000 | 0.061 | 34.4% |
| $500  | 37.6% | 0.549 | 0.558 | 0.030 | 45.2% |
| $1000 | 29.0% | 0.433 | 0.279 | 0.015 | 56.7% |
| $2500 | 22.1% | 0.325 | 0.112 | 0.006 | 67.5% |
| $5000 | 18.8% | 0.262 | 0.056 | 0.003 | 73.8% |

Naive fill (`naiveFill()` — "the stake fills in full at the posted ask") is
1.0 by construction at every stake. The real posted `ask_size`, walked by
`simulateFill()`, delivers a **mean of 0.549** at a $500 stake and **0.262**
at $5000 — real captured Polymarket depth would have absorbed roughly half a
$500 stake and about a quarter of a $5000 one, on the median real snapshot in
this dataset. This is measured directly from `bid_size`/`ask_size` this
database has been storing since 2026-08-29; nothing about it is assumed.

Real executable size **at the touch alone** (`ask_size × best_ask`, n=65,624):
p10 = \$15.20, median = \$278.88, p90 = \$17,070.09, mean = \$10,440.67 (a few
very deep futures markets pull the mean well above the median — the
distribution is heavily right-skewed, which is itself part of the finding:
typical size is thin, a handful of markets are not).

The ten thinnest real $500-stake snapshots are almost all single-player
props and futures trading at extreme prices (0.4¢–3.7¢) with resting size
under 20 contracts — fill ratios of 0.04%–0.15%. These are not an
adversarial selection; they are the actual worst rows in the actual table.

## What this does and doesn't settle

- **Settles**: on real captured Polymarket books, "assume the full stake
  fills at the posted price" is not a rounding error — at realistic stake
  sizes ($500+) it overstates achievable size by 45–74% on average. This is
  the honest number the naive live-edge.js / polymarket.js reporting-tier
  approach has been implicitly assuming away.
- **Does not settle**: what the average fill *price* would have been beyond
  the touch, or whether the same shape holds at levels 2+ — the stored table
  cannot say. Migration 044 (file only, not applied) is what would let a
  future capture answer that.
