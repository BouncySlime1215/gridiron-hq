## 2. Problems against UI-STANDARD.md

1. **Two design systems, not one** (violates rule 1 "One system"). `components/PageState.tsx` (`PageLoading`, `PageError`, `EmptyState`, `PageData`) and `components/ui/DesignSystem.tsx` (its own `EmptyState`, `ErrorState`, `Skeleton`, `Card`, `StatTile`…) both exist and overlap — two different `EmptyState` components with different props (`PageState.tsx:31` vs `DesignSystem.tsx:86`). Pages pick whichever was imported at authoring time (`Teams.tsx` uses `PageState`'s; `MyTeam.tsx` panels roll their own "Loading…" text instead of using either).
2. **No shared vocabulary components exist yet.** UI-STANDARD names `OddsLadder`, `SignalChip`, `TagPill`, `ScenarioCard`, `ReportCard`, `ReceiptCard`, `GuessLabel`, `EvidenceDrawer`, `OneTapAction` as the shared components (rule 10). None of these are defined anywhere in `client/src/components/ui/DesignSystem.tsx` today — the title-odds ladder on My Team, the manager tiers on Trade Brain, and the claim cards on News are each hand-built once, in page-local JSX, with their own markup and their own three-lines-or-more layout. This is stage UX-03's job, but the audit should record that it is a from-scratch build, not a refactor.
3. **Loading state is a spinner + text, not a skeleton**, contradicting rule 6 ("loading (skeleton)"). `PageState.tsx:10-17`'s `PageLoading` is a spinning ring plus a label; only `App.tsx`'s route-level `RouteSkeleton` (`App.tsx:38`) uses the `Skeleton` component. Individual panels inside My Team, Lineup, Trade Lab show literal loading sentences ("Loading your post-draft plan…", "Scouting your roster against the league…", "Searching every roster in the league…") which is closer to a console log than a designed state.
4. **No "thin" or "stale" state anywhere in the shared components.** Rule 6 requires both explicitly; `PageState.tsx` and `DesignSystem.tsx` only cover loading/error/empty. Thin-data language does appear ad hoc in page copy (Trade Brain: "a thin sample is visibly different from a measured neutral", comment at `TradeBrain.tsx:31`) but there is no reusable `GuessLabel`/thin-badge component — every page that wants to say "thin (n=3)" has to hand-roll it.
5. **Internal/engineering error text reaches the user.** `Lineup.tsx` surfaces "the fitted chance-to-play role layer is not running: nfl_availability_role_rates is missing/empty (docs/tdd/play-chance.tdd.md)" directly on the Start/Sit screen (confirmed in the desktop screenshot) — a file path and an internal table name on a card, which is explicitly called out as prohibited by rule 9 ("no file paths").
6. **Horizontal scroll survives on phone in three places**, contradicting rule 7 ("375px with no horizontal scroll"): the Leagues analysis `<table>` (`Leagues.tsx:184-185`), Trade Lab's 6-item tab strip (`TradeLab.tsx:128`, confirmed truncating to 4 visible tabs at 375px with a scrollbar), and the Teams grid's mid-word name truncation (`Teams.tsx:27`, effectively hiding words rather than scrolling to them, arguably worse).
7. **Settings page has no designed states at all** (0 hits for `PageLoading/PageError/PageData/EmptyState/Skeleton` in `Settings.tsx`) — the two sync buttons show nothing while in flight beyond a disabled state, and success/failure is a single after-the-fact sentence, not a card. Low-stakes page, but it is one of the 8 tabs and rule 6 applies to it too.
8. **No page currently states title odds as the first line** the way My Team's "Your title odds right now" card does (confirmed in screenshot: Championship 2.4%, Make Playoffs 35%, Expected Record 5.91W) — this card is the one place in the whole app that already matches "one currency… title odds first" (rule 2). It's on My Team only; Trade Lab and Trade Brain, which are meant to share that currency per TRADE-MACHINE-MASTER.md section 4, still lead with rosters/managers/points, not odds deltas.
9. **The one currency isn't consistent even within My Team.** The Post-Draft Action Plan (self-scout ppg deltas) and the title-odds ladder sit in the same tab but speak two different units (points-per-game rank vs. odds percentage) with no bridge line between them — closer to "feature-first" (points, tables) than "decision-first" (what should I do, and why), which is the exact gap UI-REVAMP.md names as the reason for the whole program.
10. **"My team" is not a tab.** See IA doc (UX-02) — TRADE-MACHINE-MASTER.md section 4 and UI-STANDARD's own "Surfaces" list both describe My team as one of the 8 tabs; the shipped nav (`navigation.ts:28-34`) has no `/my-team` entry — it's a sub-view inside League Hub (`LeagueHub.tsx:9,17`), and the standalone `/my-team` route is a redirect (`App.tsx:142`) into that sub-view. The odds ladder, doomsday cards, and luck ledger promised for "My team" in both planning docs are one click deeper than the nav implies.

## 3. Benchmark (public pages only, via WebSearch; no logins made, no paid tools used)

| Product | What it does best (for trades / start-sit / alerts) | Steal | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sleeper** | Trade Center: build/counter/manage offers in one place; "exploding offers" that auto-expire if the other manager doesn't respond; a trade block for signaling openness to move a player; multi-team trades. [Fantasy football on Sleeper](https://sleeper.com/fantasy-football), [Sleeper App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/sleeper-sports/id987367543) | The exploding-offer pattern maps directly onto Trade Brain's "offers out" + "counters in" — a visible countdown turns a stale offer into a decision, not a state | Sleeper's trade block is a flat list with no scoring — Gridiron already has better signal (lineup_signals, manager archetypes) and shouldn't flatten it back down to a bare list |
| **ESPN Fantasy** | Start% (how many leagues start this player) as a fast public-consensus signal; trade review shows opponent-adjusted projections. [ESPN Trade Review](https://fantasy.espn.com/football/tradereview) | The single start% number is a good "everyone else's answer" anchor to put beside Gridiron's own model number on Start/Sit, cheap trust-builder | ESPN's UI is dense with tables and low on "what to do" — exactly the feature-first pattern UI-REVAMP.md is trying to escape; don't copy the layout, just the one metric |
| **FantasyPros Trade Analyzer** | Syncs the real league, takes uneven trades (3-for-2 etc.), and reduces the whole trade to one weighted score of "who improves." [FantasyPros Trade Analyzer](https://www.fantasypros.com/nfl/myplaybook/trade-analyzer.php) | The single weighted score is close to Gridiron's title-odds delta — validates that "one number, one verdict" is the right shape for Trade Lab's evaluate-any-deal tool | It's ranking-based (100+ experts), not simulation-based — no odds, no distribution, no "why." Don't copy the shallow "win/lose" framing without the citation Gridiron already has |
| **KeepTradeCut** | Extremely fast 3-card "Keep / Trade / Cut" input turns a values database into crowd-sourced numbers; the trade calculator itself is just two team columns and a value bar. [KeepTradeCut trade calculator](https://keeptradecut.com/trade-calculator) | The two-column "your side / their side" visual for building a trade is the clearest layout pattern seen in this benchmark set — worth reusing for Trade Lab's "Mock a trade" tool | Crowd-sourced values have no cited "why" and no error bars — the opposite of UI-STANDARD rule 2's "the why with its cited number"; don't adopt the value-only framing |
| **Underdog** | Clean, minimalist screens; drafts optimize automatically after the draft (no in-season management) — praised for simplicity and fast navigation. [Underdog Drafts](https://www.underdogsports.com/games/drafts) | The minimalism itself: Underdog succeeds by removing most of the decisions Gridiron's app is built around (start/sit, trades) — a reminder that every extra control on a Gridiron card is a cost, not a neutral addition | Underdog has nothing to steal for trades or start/sit specifically — it's a different product (best ball); don't over-index on it beyond the visual-density lesson |
| **Robinhood (portfolio-app pattern)** | Cards represent one position each: one big bolded number, one accent color reserved for the single most important action on the screen, tap-to-expand from the card's own position. [Google Design: Robinhood](https://design.google/library/robinhood-investing-material) | Directly matches UI-STANDARD rule 3 ("three lines per card… nothing else") and rule 5 (color means one thing) — Robinhood's discipline about using its green for exactly one action per screen is the cleanest real-world proof this pattern works at scale | Robinhood hides real risk behind a friendly number (a known criticism) — Gridiron's guess/thin labels (rule 2, rule 6) exist specifically to avoid that failure mode; don't chase Robinhood's confidence at the cost of honesty about thin data |
